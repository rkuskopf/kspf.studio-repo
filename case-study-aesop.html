Bridging the Cultural Context Gap in Global E-commerce

Aesop’s brand is built on a "less is more" philosophy. However, global performance data revealed a major friction point: users were dropping off at the very first step of the checkout journey. Surprisingly, Japan had the highest drop-off rates globally. The challenge was to diagnose why a minimalist interface—which traditionally reduces friction—was actually causing it in certain high-value markets.

The problem When minimalism becomes ambiguity
  • Performance data showed a clear anomaly: Japan had the highest checkout abandonment globally, despite strong brand affinity and demand.
  • At first glance, the interface appeared “clean” and friction‑free. In practice, it was asking customers to proceed without enough reassurance — particularly in moments involving gifting, delivery certainty, and service quality.
  • The core challenge was not usability in isolation, but a mismatch between interface philosophy and cultural expectations.
